Sora Kang 강소라

Sora Kang is a Ph.D. candidate in Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) at HCI+D Lab, Seoul National University, advised by Prof. Joonhwan Lee. She holds a Master of Design (MDes) in HCI from University of California, Berkeley.

Grounded in her years as an actress, her research focuses on how to enhance collaborative artistic processes using AI. Her work covers two main areas: how generative AI can augment human creativity and thought, and how AI mediates communication across generations, cultures, and digital contexts.

Research

A research thread that grew out of my background as an actor. I study how generative AI can support improvisation, scriptwriting, and character development in actor training.

Actor's Note: Examining the Role of AI-Generated Questions in Character Journaling for Actor Training

S. Kang, J. Zoh, H. Kim, H. Park, H. Lim, J. Lee

Instead of writing for actors, what if an AI asked them better questions? Actor's Note reframes LLMs as maieutic partners — companions that prompt reflection on script, role, and rehearsal, rather than generating text on the actor's behalf. Over two weeks of journaling, actors found their way deeper into their characters than they would alone.

Theatrical Language Processing: Exploring AI-Augmented Improvisational Acting and Scriptwriting with LLMs

S. Kang, J. Lee

When actors improvise with an AI co-writer, the AI's unreliability turns out to be its strength — irregular, unexpected scripts push actors to think faster and create more. But when the AI gets too detailed, it crowds out the actor's own interpretation. A look at how AI can be a generative partner in acting, and where it should leave room.

Analyzing Human Heuristics and Strategies in Everyday Decision-Making Conversations for Conversational AI Design

S. Kang, S. Jeon, J. Eun, K. Lee, C. Song, M. Joo, J. Lee

People don't make everyday decisions the way decision theorists do. We hedge, defer, change the subject, ask the other person what they think. This paper looks at the messy, social heuristics people actually use — and what conversational AI gets wrong when it tries to "help" us decide.

ExplainitAI: When Do We Trust Artificial Intelligence?

S. Kang, A. E. Potinteu, N. Said

Do Koreans and Germans trust AI the same way? Koreans trusted it more — but everyone's trust dropped the moment the topic got political. Explainability matters, but what the AI is talking about matters just as much.

Designing Transparent AI-Mediated Language Support for Intergenerational Family Communication

S. Kang, Y. Hwang, J. Lee

Parents and kids increasingly speak different languages — slang, idioms, references that don't translate across generations. This work asks whether AI can bridge that gap, and finds something unexpected: families trust the AI more when they can see both the original and the translation. Hiding the original feels like being talked over.

Acting

I began my acting career in musical theater as a child, majored in musical acting at School of Performing Arts, Seoul, and later expanded my work to the screen. Acting diverse characters and putting myself in their shoes has nurtured my emotional range and deepened my understanding of people. Through years of reading scripts many times over, analyzing each line, developing the subtext beneath it, building characters, and constructing worlds on stage, I came to see language as something that informs and transforms human behavior and social interaction — a perspective that now grounds my research and creative practice.
